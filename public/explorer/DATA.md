# Universe Explorer: data credits and limits

## Stars and constellations

- **8,723 stars** adapted from **David Nash / Astronomy Nexus, [HYG Database v4.1](https://github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database/tree/c7f7f883fe678cc7680169a50ccd7dcc49b060ce/hyg)**, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). HYG combines Hipparcos astrometry and photometry with the Yale Bright Star and Gliese nearby-star catalogs, among other corrections documented upstream. We selected observed stars with visual magnitude V ≤ 6.5 or catalog distance ≤ 3 pc, plus selected tour stars and diagram endpoints. We retained HYG's J2000 Cartesian coordinates, catalog V, absolute V, and B−V (where present), remapped HIP/HYG identifiers and added a few localized labels. Absolute V is derived from distance and apparent V; it is not an independent luminosity measurement. We omit HYG's missing/dubious parallax sentinel (100,000 pc) rather than guessing a position. Remaining distances are still estimates, with some large errors for distant stars and multiple systems. Positions and catalog brightness stay fixed with the simulation date; proper motion, variability, extinction and flyby light travel are not modeled.
- **88 representative line figures** adapted from the **Stellarium team, [Modern sky culture](https://github.com/Stellarium/stellarium/tree/e233fa9b3d21e2cafe9c168e6710b627532a7dd4/skycultures/modern)**, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). We converted HIP polylines into pairs, localized names, and omitted edges touching seven HIP entries with no usable HYG distance (5165, 22783, 31216, 33165, 54463, 89341 and 92202). Neither stars nor replacement line endpoints were invented. The IAU standardizes 88 constellation **names and sky regions**, not stick figures. These figures are one convention, not official IAU lines, and the paired stars are usually unrelated physically.
- The adapted star payload (`public/explorer/stars.json`) and derived constellation/featured-star index (`src/explorer/catalog-index.json`) are distributed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). This does not relicense the surrounding app code. Rebuild both with `python scripts\build-explorer-catalog.py` (Python 3.10+, fetches the two pinned inputs; optionally provide the HYG CSV and Stellarium JSON paths in that order).

## Curated marks

- **M42, M45, M1, M31 and M33:** rounded distance and integrated apparent visual brightness from [NASA's Hubble Messier Catalog](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/explore-the-night-sky/hubble-messier-catalog/); representative ICRS center coordinates from [SIMBAD, CDS Strasbourg](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/). An extended object's integrated magnitude does not predict naked-eye surface brightness, and its center is only a navigation point.
- **Sagittarius A\*:** SIMBAD sky position, rounded from its radio counterpart, and the [GRAVITY Collaboration geometric distance estimate](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201935656), 8,178 pc. There is no ordinary visible-light magnitude for this marker; `Infinity` represents unavailable brightness, not a measurement.
- **3C 273:** rounded [SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=3C%20273) sky position and variable optical V ≈ 12.9. The plotting radius is a model-dependent ~652 Mpc radial comoving distance using redshift ~0.158 and a flat cosmology (H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc, Ωₘ = 0.3); it is not a parallax, luminosity distance, or light-travel time.
- **Proxima Centauri b, TRAPPIST-1 b, 51 Pegasi b and Kepler-186 f:** confirmed planets from the [NASA Exoplanet Archive Planetary Systems Composite Parameters](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/API_PSCompPars_columns.html), using `ra`, `dec`, `sy_dist`, and `sy_vmag`. Markers give the host star's direction, distance and V, **not the planet's position, size or brightness**. The host direction may differ slightly from HYG for the same star because the inputs use different astrometric solutions/epochs. The archive requests [this acknowledgment](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/acknowledge.html): This research has made use of the NASA Exoplanet Archive, which is operated by the California Institute of Technology, under contract with the National Aeronautics and Space Administration under the Exoplanet Exploration Program. No NASA, CDS or archive images or source prose are reproduced; only selected numerical facts and identifiers are used.

## Solar system and display conventions

- [Astronomy Engine 2.1.19](https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy/tree/v2.1.19), Don Cross, [MIT notice](./Astronomy-Engine-MIT.txt), supplies heliocentric geometric Sun/planet positions, geocentric geometric Moon position, the observer's sea-level rotational offset, horizon axes (including precession and nutation), and model visual magnitudes. We apply an observer-distance correction to magnitudes; phase and Saturn's ring tilt are modeled from Earth's center, not recomputed topocentrically. Earth has no Earth-view magnitude here. Sizes are mean planetary radii from [NASA NSSDC planetary fact sheets](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/), mean lunar radius from the [Moon fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html), and nominal solar radius from [IAU 2015 Resolution B3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.07674); the Sun's reference absolute V is from [Willmer 2018](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/aabfdf).
- Positions are parsecs in ICRS-aligned J2000 equatorial axes: +X is R.A. 0 h, +Y is R.A. 6 h, and +Z is north celestial. The scene origin is the Sun; local-sky directions subtract the topocentric observer from positions. There is no position-level light-time correction, relativistic deflection, stellar/planetary aberration, terrain, or atmospheric refraction. This is not a precision pointing, eclipse prediction, or navigation tool.
- The observer date advances Solar System motion and rotates the local horizon; it does not time-evolve the stellar or deep-sky catalog. Color, glow, line work, minimum pixel sizes, landmark symbols and arrival viewpoints are illustrative. Distant extended objects are not resolved and a fly-through is a visualization, not a physically timed voyage.
