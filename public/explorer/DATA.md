# Universe Explorer: data credits and limits

## Stars and constellations

The star snapshot ships with the game and is loaded from the same origin, never from a live catalog API. Browsers with Cache Storage can reuse an already downloaded snapshot if the page itself loads on a weak connection. A completely offline first load is not supported.

- **8,723 stars** adapted from **David Nash / Astronomy Nexus, [HYG Database v4.1](https://github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database/tree/c7f7f883fe678cc7680169a50ccd7dcc49b060ce/hyg)**, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). HYG combines Hipparcos astrometry and photometry with the Yale Bright Star and Gliese nearby-star catalogs, among other corrections documented upstream. We selected observed stars with visual magnitude V ≤ 6.5 or catalog distance ≤ 3 pc, plus selected tour stars and diagram endpoints. We retained HYG's J2000 Cartesian coordinates, catalog V, absolute V, and B−V (where present), remapped HIP/HYG identifiers and added a few localized labels. Absolute V is derived from distance and apparent V; it is not an independent luminosity measurement. We omit HYG's missing/dubious parallax sentinel (100,000 pc) rather than guessing a position. Remaining distances are still estimates, with some large errors for distant stars and multiple systems. Positions and catalog brightness stay fixed with the simulation date; proper motion, variability, extinction and flyby light travel are not modeled.
- **88 representative line figures** adapted from the **Stellarium team, [Modern sky culture](https://github.com/Stellarium/stellarium/tree/e233fa9b3d21e2cafe9c168e6710b627532a7dd4/skycultures/modern)**, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). We converted HIP polylines into pairs, localized names, and omitted edges touching seven HIP entries with no usable HYG distance (5165, 22783, 31216, 33165, 54463, 89341 and 92202). Neither stars nor replacement line endpoints were invented. The IAU standardizes 88 constellation **names and sky regions**, not stick figures. These figures are one convention, not official IAU lines, and the paired stars are usually unrelated physically.
- The adapted star payload (`public/explorer/stars.json`) and derived constellation/featured-star index (`src/explorer/catalog-index.json`) are distributed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). This does not relicense the surrounding app code. Rebuild both with `python scripts\build-explorer-catalog.py` (Python 3.10+, fetches the two pinned inputs; optionally provide the HYG CSV and Stellarium JSON paths in that order).

- **Traditional Chinese star names** in `src/explorer/star-names-zh.ts` are adapted from Karrie Berglund, Sun Shuwei and contributors to [Stellarium’s Chinese sky culture](https://github.com/Stellarium/stellarium/tree/e233fa9b3d21e2cafe9c168e6710b627532a7dd4/skycultures/chinese), based primarily on Yi Shitong’s *Chinese and Western Contrast Star Chart and Catalogue 1950.0*. The lookup selects the first unambiguous traditional name for each matching HIP identifier and removes source bibliography markers. The adapted lookup data is [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

## Curated marks

- **M42, M45, M1, M31 and M33:** rounded distance and integrated apparent visual brightness from [NASA's Hubble Messier Catalog](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/explore-the-night-sky/hubble-messier-catalog/); representative ICRS center coordinates from [SIMBAD, CDS Strasbourg](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/). An extended object's integrated magnitude does not predict naked-eye surface brightness, and its center is only a navigation point.
- **Sagittarius A\*:** SIMBAD sky position, rounded from its radio counterpart, and the [GRAVITY Collaboration geometric distance estimate](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201935656), 8,178 pc. There is no ordinary visible-light magnitude for this marker; `Infinity` represents unavailable brightness, not a measurement.
- **3C 273:** rounded [SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=3C%20273) sky position and variable optical V ≈ 12.9. The plotting radius is a model-dependent ~652 Mpc radial comoving distance using redshift ~0.158 and a flat cosmology (H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc, Ωₘ = 0.3); it is not a parallax, luminosity distance, or light-travel time.
- **Proxima Centauri b, TRAPPIST-1 b, 51 Pegasi b and Kepler-186 f:** confirmed planets from the [NASA Exoplanet Archive Planetary Systems Composite Parameters](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/API_PSCompPars_columns.html), using `ra`, `dec`, `sy_dist`, and `sy_vmag`. Markers give the host star's direction, distance and V, **not the planet's position, size or brightness**. The host direction may differ slightly from HYG for the same star because the inputs use different astrometric solutions/epochs. The archive requests [this acknowledgment](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/acknowledge.html): This research has made use of the NASA Exoplanet Archive, which is operated by the California Institute of Technology, under contract with the National Aeronautics and Space Administration under the Exoplanet Exploration Program. No NASA, CDS or archive images or source prose are reproduced; only selected numerical facts and identifiers are used.

## Solar system and display conventions

- [Astronomy Engine 2.1.19](https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy/tree/v2.1.19), Don Cross, [MIT notice](./Astronomy-Engine-MIT.txt), supplies heliocentric geometric Sun/planet positions, geocentric geometric Moon position, the observer's sea-level rotational offset, horizon axes (including precession and nutation), and model visual magnitudes. We apply an observer-distance correction to magnitudes; phase and Saturn's ring tilt are modeled from Earth's center, not recomputed topocentrically. Earth has no Earth-view magnitude here. Sizes are mean planetary radii from [NASA NSSDC planetary fact sheets](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/), mean lunar radius from the [Moon fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html), and nominal solar radius from [IAU 2015 Resolution B3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.07674); the Sun's reference absolute V is from [Willmer 2018](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/aabfdf).
- Positions are parsecs in ICRS-aligned J2000 equatorial axes: +X is R.A. 0 h, +Y is R.A. 6 h, and +Z is north celestial. The scene origin is the Sun; local-sky directions subtract the topocentric observer from positions. Outside the separate Pale Blue Dot reconstruction, there is no position-level light-time correction, relativistic deflection, stellar/planetary aberration, terrain, or atmospheric refraction. This is not a precision pointing, eclipse prediction, or navigation tool.
- The observer date advances Solar System motion and rotates the local horizon; it does not time-evolve the stellar or deep-sky catalog. Glow, line work, minimum point sizes, landmark symbols, and arrival viewpoints are illustrative. A fly-through is a visualization, not a physically timed voyage.

## Observational imagery

The four nearby views are telescope composites, not naked-eye appearances. Night Atlas places each plate at its published equatorial center and orientation from Earth, uses its published field and the atlas’s catalog distance for scale, and reorients the flat plate for virtual approach. The plate is not a 3D reconstruction, and colors/brightness are not photometric. Offscreen plates are culled, and highly oblique views fade out rather than stretching the image. Plates are resampled to at most 960 px, darkened slightly where needed, edge-feathered and WebP-encoded for display; only M31 is cropped. Original images and credits:

- **Milky Way:** [NASA’s Scientific Visualization Studio, Ernie Wright, *Deep Star Maps 2020*](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851/), [Galactic-coordinate background](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004800/a004851/milkyway_2020_4k_gal_print.jpg), corrected 2021 edition. This is a visualization of observed catalog stars (chiefly Gaia DR2 in this background variant), not a photograph or an invented dust model. Empty/darker lanes are associated with extinction in visible-light star counts. The published map omits bright Hipparcos and Tycho stars, avoiding overlap with the atlas’s interactive bright stars. The local derivative removes a small black-level pedestal, mildly adjusts contrast and is WebP-encoded. It reprojects the map using the ICRS Galactic basis (north up in the source, Galactic longitude increasing left), rotates with the viewer, and fades away outside the solar neighborhood. The visible bulge is the optical Galactic-center region: it does not depict Sagittarius A* itself. The unmarked NASA SVS imagery is reusable for educational simulations under [NASA Media Usage Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/); no NASA endorsement is implied.

## Spacecraft viewpoints and arrival notes

- **Voyager 1, Voyager 2, and New Horizons:** packaged public-domain [NASA/JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/) geometric heliocentric state vectors in ICRF equatorial axes (AU and AU/day, UT epochs). The [JPL data-use FAQ](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/faq.html) explains reuse. The bundled snapshot records the exact queries and trajectory solutions. Yearly states span 1990–2035 for Voyager 1 and 2 and 2020–2035 for New Horizons. Within those outbound segments the app interpolates from position and velocity; outside them it uses the nearest endpoint and displays that epoch. This is a model (including predicted states), not live tracking or a reconstruction of the earlier flybys. `python scripts\build-explorer-spacecraft.py` refreshes the snapshot; the browser never queries Horizons.
- **Pale Blue Dot:** the historical Voyager 1 viewpoint uses a Horizons state at [NASA’s documented exposure time](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1s-pale-blue-dot/), 1990-02-14 04:48 UTC. This view alone accounts approximately for Earth’s light travel time. It does not reproduce the original camera optics, pointing corrections, or stray sunlight. The Earth marker is enlarged enough to be seen at screen resolution.
- **Arrival facts** are short bilingual summaries with a link to the source included in each note. Omitted masses and ages are not guessed.

## Illustrative phenomena

- **Sagittarius A\*:** a rotating hot-flow sketch inspired by the [Event Horizon Telescope ring and shadow](https://eventhorizontelescope.org/blog/astronomers-reveal-first-image-black-hole-heart-our-galaxy), not a confirmed jet.
- **Crab pulsar:** a torus, outward-moving wisps, and opposing jets, following [Chandra’s observations](https://chandra.harvard.edu/photo/2002/0052/animations.html) and the projected axis in [Ng & Romani](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.4168). The illustration is distinct from the credited visible-light nebula plate.
- **3C 273:** a predominantly one-sided structure inspired by the [Hubble images](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-takes-the-closest-ever-look-at-a-quasar/) and the [published jet position angle](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March02/Courvoisier/Cour6.html).
- The effects are original drawings; scale, color, and motion are illustrative rather than calibrated or predictive. Animation stops offscreen or in a hidden tab, and a still remains available with reduced motion or the animation setting off. The app uses canvas timing and frame gaps, not battery or CPU telemetry, for its performance suggestion.
