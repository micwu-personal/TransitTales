The root entry extends the coastal world into an original side-view, dimensional station cutaway. Deep green ink, seafoam surroundings, ivory floors, planted landings and restrained operational colors inherit the metro identity. This is an authored SVG scene, not an interactive 3D map.

Each explains the actual play loop and offers one full-width action. The artwork itself is decorative, with a localized text equivalent; no essential action relies on deciphering a picture or color. On phones the scene precedes the same two departures in normal document order, without horizontal page overflow. Shared language preference, native links, visible keyboard focus and reduced-motion behavior keep the collection straightforward.

Destination signs combine color and localized text across five locales. The mall occupies the current dynamic viewport, with a unified building bench, visible puzzle chooser and compact adaptive shopping row/column. Essential controls need no popup and never scroll the scene away. The shared HUD reserves scene space from actual control geometry; open space continues to accept camera gestures.

Strollers avoid escalators, couriers avoid elevators and height-sensitive visitors avoid floor 4 and above. Accessories, badges and route diagnostics explain these needs. Untimed planning and recoverable waves invite experimentation. Editing and retrying clears old feedback; hidden/exit/mute cancel the flourish without replaying it on return.

The building blocks, play/pause and bench share one floating bottom group; reverse, mirror, delete, undo, reset and hint form the next row beneath the blocks. Compact shopping requests sit directly above the bench in tall/narrow layouts and become a right-hand column when horizontal room permits; only genuinely overflowing requests scroll internally. The puzzle chooser occupies a compact strip above the scene. Paired 3D/2D buttons move to the title bar immediately left of language selection, retaining full localized accessible names. Wheelchair/balloon rules begin as icons; related explanations, request diagnostics and hints open a small anchored nonmodal popout rather than changing the list's height. Explicit close/Escape restores trigger focus; outside interaction dismisses without blocking the underlying control. Dynamic scene insets follow actual header, puzzle strip, dock and shopping placement, including translated labels. Drag, cancellation and multi-finger guards separate camera gestures from construction; a new tap is not delayed. Native projected landing/piece buttons in 3D and keyboard-operable SVG nodes provide alternatives to canvas picking. Close-ups hide offscreen anchors and their labels/leaders; zoom-out or reset restores them rather than accumulating edge controls. Focus, live feedback, five-locale labels and reduced-motion behavior remain intentional. Traditional Mongolian explanations and signs use vertical script while compact operational controls stay horizontal. This is real 3D presentation, not a physical crowd or escalator simulator. Floors are visual architecture, not implicit walkable connections: only placed pieces join landings.

## Melody Train: real notation, a singing journey

**Surface mode:** Operate. The incumbent seafoam, ivory, green-ink and coral world expands into a musical railway workshop rather than a new visual brand. An original station/train illustration marks a real playable concourse departure. Desktop lesson tickets form a quiet left rail; phones use a contained horizontal lesson strip. One short mission, an orbitable miniature railway, the real five-line staff and a labeled editing bench establish the loop: change a note, listen, observe, revise. The scenery illustrates contour only; notation remains an independent authoritative reference rather than five fictional pitch tracks.

Authored SVG clefs anchor the correct lines; ledger lines, real rests, stems, flags, dots, keys and barlines teach ordinary notation. Large named note targets, native selectors, selectable stop buttons, up/down/Delete and stable focus support keyboard and touch. Free composition stays available without unlocking; hints expose the target without modifying the draft. Separate ear questions hide the mystery score/frequency until an answer. Quiet pure sine synthesis and an explicit A4=440 Hz reference avoid competing sound, misleading vibrato or absolute-pitch promises.

The genuine lazy Babylon and lightweight SVG railway consume the same snapshot and AudioContext-driven timeline. Views/languages preserve score, selection and playback; 3D failure returns safely to SVG. Reduced motion keeps the train at the active station. Horizontal score scrolling preserves real spacing without overflowing the page. Traditional Mongolian prose uses the bundled vertical font while musical symbols and score geometry remain horizontal; translations/device behavior retain explicit review limits. Unavailable audio is not called successful listening, and hidden/exit stops sound without auto-resume.

## Rush Hour Rescue: a little city worth looking after

**Product thesis:** Make care visible. Select a waiting crowd, send a relief train, and watch it cross a miniature coastal city. The city is the experience, not decoration behind a dashboard. The game title is **Transit Tales**; its intended project identity is **TransitTales**.

**Audience and use:** Children and curious casual players on desktop, keyboard, or touch. Start a short shift, find a busy station, dispatch, and notice the consequences. All five written locales have equal standing; switching languages never restarts a shift.

**Visual world:** A warm, original coastal diorama under soft daylight, with genuine 3D and 2.5D SVG views. Seafoam water (`#a9cfca`), an ivory shell (`#f5f3eb`), planted pale-green land, ochre and slate roofs, and deep green ink (`#284d47`) establish a quiet city. Coral and blue retain their operational line meanings. Buildings have separate front walls, side walls, roofs, windows and offset ground shadows. The harbor, river crossing, park paths, stadium, museum and airport give each neighborhood a distinct place. The same world has authored SVG and procedural Babylon 3D presentations; no stock images, imported sets or franchise marks. The genuine miniature city has planted neighborhoods, station architecture, raised tracks, water and moving three-car trains. Mouse or one-finger drag orbits on open space; wheel or two-finger pinch zooms. The scene toolbar offers localized ≥44px + / − and reset controls, hidden in 2D. Shared bounded camera helpers preserve magnification through viewport/orbit refitting without changing game state. Drag, cancellation and multi-finger guards prevent unintended station selections. Station labels/leaders hide when their projected anchors leave the frame and recover on zoom-out or reset.

**Hierarchy and composition:** A dynamic-viewport coastal scene leads, with a compact floating brand/language/help row, short shift counters and a persistent pause/speed dock. The same paired 3D/2D and zoom/reset controls used in the atrium sit just above the dock. A collapsible, internally scrolling dispatch panel contains scenario, mission and selected-platform relief actions; a floating inspector replaces that panel while examining passengers. Desktop reserves scene room for the panel; narrow viewports use bounded overlays. Help contains the legend, and results appear in a bounded overlay rather than pushing the scene out of view.

**Typography and spacing:** System UI text with Segoe UI on Windows and native Chinese fallbacks; restrained 650-weight headings, plain readable supporting copy, and tabular counters. Large groups are separated by space or a fine hairline rather than heavy containers. Chinese headings use natural tracking and more line height. Buttons wrap rather than clip translated text.

**Signature behavior:** Three-car trains move continuously between simulation steps, follow the railway direction, and carry a small upright rider badge. A selected station uses a coral platform outline; high queues use warm queue badges, and helpers tint the platform green. Train interpolation stops during pause, ready/finished states or reduced-motion preference. These views do not change simulation time or rules.

**Responsive and inclusive behavior:** Desktop, phone, tablet, landscape and arbitrary split views keep one dynamic-height game surface. Floating controls adapt to measured header/dock sizes; only the panel or SVG map scrolls, not the document. The SVG retains a minimum width of 680px so station labels and hit areas do not become microscopic; the contained city can be swiped horizontally, with a localized hint. Keyboard focus brings offscreen stations into view through native overflow scrolling. Selectable stations have button roles, localized names, pressed state and Enter/Space activation. Form controls have 44px minimum heights, visible focus, and honest disabled explanations. Queues wrap; result controls wrap; the page itself must not overflow horizontally.

**Renderer boundary:** `view.ts` makes detached, deeply frozen renderer-neutral snapshots containing station identities/status, line stop identities/colors, and normalized train positions/segment progress. `city.ts` owns presentation coordinates and coastal scenery; it does not read simulation coordinates. `renderer.ts` implements `CityRenderer.mount(host, onSelect)`, `render(snapshot)` and `dispose()`. The SVG renderer owns its nodes/listeners and maps normalized positions to its projection. The shipped lazy Babylon renderer consumes the same contract without changing simulation. Native projected station/train buttons supplement canvas picking, and WebGL initialization/context failures return to SVG without resetting the shift or interrupting its broadcast. Main owns gameplay, localization, pause/speed, storage and frame scheduling.

**Dual-view standard:** The default genuine 3D city fits its actual viewport, while the complete 2D map retains contained horizontal scrolling. View changes preserve time, pause, selection and broadcast playback. Upcoming mini-games should ship both 2D and genuine 3D through detached snapshots, accessible selection controls and `mount/render/dispose`, with reduced-motion equivalents and failure fallback.

**Honest limits:** No arbitrary-city UI/importer, automatic real-city scenery generator or real-world map data is implemented. The adapter can remap coordinates and accept different network data; the shipped scenery is still authored for one fictional ten-station city. Passenger queues and train cars are visual aggregates, not individual route agents in a physically modeled city. Browser visual and touch QA remains a separate validation step.

## Beijing Journey: trace a route, then ride it

**Surface mode:** Operate while planning; Experience while riding. An original Beijing schematic and first-person carriage/station world extend the collection’s warm ivory, green-ink and sunlit miniature identity, with a quiet red-roof city connection rather than a generic game card.

**Composition:** The map owns the planning viewport. Search, fit, pan and zoom preserve the full 28-service network; a collapsible floating planner makes origin, destination, chosen lines, transfers, passenger needs and alternatives readable without moving the map away. An illustrated passenger mission and three-step route/board/arrival sequence put actual route choices ahead of empty instructions. Manual line construction remains in a discoverable workshop that opens when a map/search station is selected; mission details and tutorials remain optional. The selected map tab has explicit contrasting hover/pressed styles. A compact trip dock keeps pause, 1×/5×/15× speed, broadcast enable/mute and replay reachable. Route milestones reflect actual visited stations; a completion postcard presents real score and personal best rather than inventing achievements. Ride views use genuine lazy Babylon 3D or a complete lightweight 2D first-person presentation behind frozen snapshots. Captions/events give the passenger’s journey a readable rhythm rather than a hidden simulation.

**Map readability:** Rails and screen-sized station markers lead. Bilingual station names retain a constant 12px Chinese / 10px English size at every zoom; a thin halo protects letters without turning labels into large opaque blocks. Deterministic screen-space placement avoids label overlaps, rail corridors and station markers, with selected/current names taking precedence over route endpoints, interchanges and ordinary names. Crowded names defer until space opens; all 426 station controls remain searchable and keyboard reachable. Resizing and touch zoom relayout labels without changing route state.

**Product truth:** Six rounds and a sandbox use a dated 30-named-line Beijing urban-rail pack, not a live navigation feed. Through-services and the airport terminal circuit preserve real topology; explicit external walking distinguishes exiting/re-entering from paid-area transfers. Times, crowding, toilets, access and incidents are game estimates. Original schematic positions derive from licensed OSM data, with visible attribution and disclosed interpolation. Beijing uses bundled recorded phrase parts and actual Mandarin station/service identities, not installed device speech or invented localized name pronunciations. Boarding activates one reusable media element unless the player muted it; an active logical sentence finishes before the newest pending event, including at fast-forward speeds. Scene/planner replay can retry blocked or missing media, with visible failure guidance and persistent captions. Both Mongolian scripts share Mongolia Mongolian phrases. Recordings are original game scripts, not official announcements, and compositional MP3 pauses and physical-device review are disclosed. Written locales have equal standing; actual station names are not invented to imply official Japanese/Mongolian translations.

# Legacy: Build a World design record

**Product thesis:** A living miniature stage, not a menu of disconnected puzzles. Children choose a world, make it with their hands, and watch it answer.

**Audience and use:** Primary-school builders on desktop or touch screens. First-run invitation offers five doors; each themed stage gives a short goal and honest feedback. Creative Studio removes the goal entirely.

**Visual direction:** A sunlit tabletop theater with paper-card controls and softly modeled clay shapes. A blue drafting sky gives breathing room; warm cream cards, butter yellow calls to action, coral details, mint success states, and dark ink outlines keep controls legible. The camera, textured stages, little residents, orbit guides, and translucent placement preview make the building surface feel alive without relying on stock assets or familiar toy designs.

**Hierarchy:** World chooser → world mission → uninterrupted 3D stage → reachable shape/color/edit bench → undo/save utilities. The mission stays visible, while results occupy a small nonblocking card so the child can see reactions and continue building. Text labels accompany shapes and buttons; focus indicators, accessible names, live status feedback, and reduced-motion CSS support different input needs.

**Responsive behavior:** Desktop tools flank the stage; on narrow screens, the mission shrinks, utility actions become labeled-by-tooltip icons, and the bench becomes a horizontally scrolling shape tray plus compact color/edit groups. Touch gestures orbit and pinch-zoom the stage; shape selection followed by tap avoids requiring drag-and-drop on touch.

**Originality and portability:** Geometry and scenery are assembled from primitives at runtime. There are no branded characters, imported set designs, stock illustrations, or third-party audio. Portable rules and snapshot validation live separately from Babylon rendering and browser storage; no platform shell is bundled.

## Metro interiors: stay with the journey
A nonmodal floating cutaway keeps dispatch available while opening a platform or carriage, without scrolling the city out of the viewport. Pale-green structure, ivory floors, seafoam windows, operational coral/blue trim and an overhead destination sign extend the coastal world. Small original passenger figures use live IDs/counts, bounded to 24 with an explicit remaining count. A readable broadcast rail pairs captions with play/retry, mute and status. Mobile stacks scene and broadcast without horizontal page overflow. Keyboard station/train activation, focus restoration and Escape support exploration without trapping focus.

Real-service captions are kept separate from a clearly labeled broadcast studio for hypothetical non-stopping-service and sample notices. Recorded full sentences retain one voice per spoken language across devices; captions and failure states never disappear when audio is unavailable. Automatic journey updates finish the active sentence and keep only the newest pending announcement, which starts on completion even without another simulation event. A separate pending line leaves the main caption aligned with the speaking recording. Explicit context/replay/language changes may replace audio, but mute, close and page exit clear the queue. Reusing one media element keeps the mobile authorization path consistent. Snapshot and playback seams leave room for richer renderers and refined recordings without modifying dispatch simulation.
