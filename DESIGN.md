# Transit Tales — design record

## Collection concourse: choose a place, not a tile

The root entry extends the coastal world into an original side-view, dimensional station cutaway. A sunlit glass-canopy atrium and coral metro carriage occupy one connected illustration, rather than separate game thumbnails. Deep green ink, seafoam surroundings, ivory floors, planted landings and restrained operational colors inherit the metro identity. This is an authored SVG scene, not an interactive 3D map.

The invitation leads directly to two labeled departures beside the architecture: upward to the escalator puzzle and downward to metro dispatch. Each explains the actual play loop and offers one full-width action. The artwork itself is decorative, with a localized text equivalent; no essential action relies on deciphering a picture or color. On phones the scene precedes the same two departures in normal document order, without horizontal page overflow. Future concepts and the preserved localized construction studio sit below a quiet divider and are explicitly distinguished from playable transit adventures. Shared language preference, native links, visible keyboard focus and reduced-motion behavior keep the collection straightforward.

## Escalator Orchestra: build a journey, hear it arrive

A sunlit miniature mall extends the collection's ivory floors, green ink, seafoam daylight and planted landings. The default genuine 3D atrium and complete side-view SVG fallback frame the same seven-column, four-floor route-building field. Floors, shopfronts, plants, steps, rails, lift cabins and direction arrows make the mechanism dimensional rather than imitating 3D with UI chrome. Destination signs combine color and localized text across five locales. The interface remains a quiet construction desk beside the mall, not a timer-driven arcade dashboard.

Choose a tool, then two labeled landings. Selection rings, travel arrows, route checkmarks and previews make construction visible before shoppers depart. Waiting figures show wheelchair frames and full-sized tethered balloons; independent, high-contrast request badges and an adjacent access guide explain both constraints. Striped physical low roofs, posts, height gauges and crossed-balloon symbols explain why a route needs open headroom. Route-specific warnings distinguish completed-but-inaccessible paths from unfinished ones. Fourteen puzzles include lantern detours, downward returns, shared trunks, serpentine routes, garden mazes and combined-access journeys. Untimed planning and recoverable waves invite experimentation. Optional original mellow chords and arpeggios accompany planning; volume/pause controls and visual arrival notes keep sound nonessential. Independently enabled round sounds play a short original flourish once on full success, accompanied by a brief shared musical-note celebration in both views and a static reduced-motion equivalent. Concern markers and localized destination, landing and access reasons make unhappy shoppers visible without blaming valid travellers. Editing and retrying clears old feedback; hidden/exit/mute cancel the flourish without replaying it on return.

Desktop pairs the large atrium with the tool desk; phones place controls below a screen-fitting 3D scene or horizontally swipeable 2D plan. The scene toolbar selects 3D/2D and resets the orbit camera. Native projected landing/piece buttons in 3D and keyboard-operable SVG nodes provide alternatives to canvas picking. Focus, live feedback, five-locale labels and reduced-motion behavior remain intentional. Traditional Mongolian prose and signs use vertical script while coordinate buttons stay horizontal. Renderer and language changes do not clear routes or restart waves; hidden tabs pause waves and suspend music. The root collection link remains visible.

The pure model owns placement, graph traversal, history and deterministic wave/scoring rules. Detached, deeply frozen snapshots cross into the mount/render/dispose renderer contract; display-only steps and waiting figures cannot change routes. The lazy Babylon renderer owns scene geometry, projected DOM controls, camera and cleanup; WebGL loading/context failures return to SVG without changing gameplay. Original MIDI-note-based WebAudio music has an independent gesture-gated scheduling/lifecycle module and uses no external service. This is real 3D presentation, not a physical crowd or escalator simulator. Floors are visual architecture, not implicit walkable connections: only placed pieces join landings.

## Rush Hour Rescue: a little city worth looking after

**Product thesis:** Make care visible. Select a waiting crowd, send a relief train, and watch it cross a miniature coastal city. The city is the experience, not decoration behind a dashboard. The game title is **Transit Tales**; its intended project identity is **TransitTales**.

**Audience and use:** Children and curious casual players on desktop, keyboard, or touch. Start a short shift, find a busy station, dispatch, and notice the consequences. All five written locales have equal standing; switching languages never restarts a shift.

**Visual world:** A warm, original coastal diorama under soft daylight, with genuine 3D and 2.5D SVG views. Seafoam water (`#a9cfca`), an ivory shell (`#f5f3eb`), planted pale-green land, ochre and slate roofs, and deep green ink (`#284d47`) establish a quiet city. Coral and blue retain their operational line meanings. Buildings have separate front walls, side walls, roofs, windows and offset ground shadows. The harbor, river crossing, park paths, stadium, museum and airport give each neighborhood a distinct place. The same world has authored SVG and procedural Babylon 3D presentations; no stock images, imported sets or franchise marks. The genuine miniature city has planted neighborhoods, station architecture, raised tracks, water and moving three-car trains. Drag open space to orbit and reset the view from the scene toolbar.

**Hierarchy and composition:** A compact brand/navigation bar precedes a map-led field with a short invitation and four modest shift counters. The wide city shares the first viewport with a restrained, unboxed dispatch rail: scenario → start/pause and speed → mission → selected platform and relief actions → notice/help. A line legend and state hint sit below the city. Results appear below, leaving the city in view. No oversized hero, chunky paper borders, decorative stamps or nested cards.

**Typography and spacing:** System UI text with Segoe UI on Windows and native Chinese fallbacks; restrained 650-weight headings, plain readable supporting copy, and tabular counters. Large groups are separated by space or a fine hairline rather than heavy containers. Chinese headings use natural tracking and more line height. Buttons wrap rather than clip translated text.

**Signature behavior:** Three-car trains move continuously between simulation steps, follow the railway direction, and carry a small upright rider badge. A selected station uses a coral platform outline; high queues use warm queue badges, and helpers tint the platform green. Train interpolation stops during pause, ready/finished states or reduced-motion preference. These views do not change simulation time or rules.

**Responsive and inclusive behavior:** Desktop shows the city beside the dispatch rail. Tablet-width controls become a two-column workbench below it. Phones return to a single-column desk. The SVG retains a minimum width of 680px so station labels and hit areas do not become microscopic; the contained city can be swiped horizontally, with a localized hint. Keyboard focus brings offscreen stations into view through native overflow scrolling. Selectable stations have button roles, localized names, pressed state and Enter/Space activation. Form controls have 44px minimum heights, visible focus, and honest disabled explanations. Queues wrap; result controls wrap; the page itself must not overflow horizontally.

**Renderer boundary:** `view.ts` makes detached, deeply frozen renderer-neutral snapshots containing station identities/status, line stop identities/colors, and normalized train positions/segment progress. `city.ts` owns presentation coordinates and coastal scenery; it does not read simulation coordinates. `renderer.ts` implements `CityRenderer.mount(host, onSelect)`, `render(snapshot)` and `dispose()`. The SVG renderer owns its nodes/listeners and maps normalized positions to its projection. The shipped lazy Babylon renderer consumes the same contract without changing simulation. Native projected station/train buttons supplement canvas picking, and WebGL initialization/context failures return to SVG without resetting the shift or interrupting its broadcast. Main owns gameplay, localization, pause/speed, storage and frame scheduling.

**Dual-view standard:** The default genuine 3D city fits its actual viewport, while the complete 2D map retains contained horizontal scrolling. View changes preserve time, pause, selection and broadcast playback. Upcoming mini-games should ship both 2D and genuine 3D through detached snapshots, accessible selection controls and `mount/render/dispose`, with reduced-motion equivalents and failure fallback.

**Honest limits:** No arbitrary-city UI/importer, automatic real-city scenery generator or real-world map data is implemented. The adapter can remap coordinates and accept different network data; the shipped scenery is still authored for one fictional ten-station city. Passenger queues and train cars are visual aggregates, not individual route agents in a physically modeled city. Browser visual and touch QA remains a separate validation step.

# Legacy: Build a World design record

**Product thesis:** A living miniature stage, not a menu of disconnected puzzles. Children choose a world, make it with their hands, and watch it answer.

**Audience and use:** Primary-school builders on desktop or touch screens. First-run invitation offers five doors; each themed stage gives a short goal and honest feedback. Creative Studio removes the goal entirely.

**Visual direction:** A sunlit tabletop theater with paper-card controls and softly modeled clay shapes. A blue drafting sky gives breathing room; warm cream cards, butter yellow calls to action, coral details, mint success states, and dark ink outlines keep controls legible. The camera, textured stages, little residents, orbit guides, and translucent placement preview make the building surface feel alive without relying on stock assets or familiar toy designs.

**Hierarchy:** World chooser → world mission → uninterrupted 3D stage → reachable shape/color/edit bench → undo/save utilities. The mission stays visible, while results occupy a small nonblocking card so the child can see reactions and continue building. Text labels accompany shapes and buttons; focus indicators, accessible names, live status feedback, and reduced-motion CSS support different input needs.

**Responsive behavior:** Desktop tools flank the stage; on narrow screens, the mission shrinks, utility actions become labeled-by-tooltip icons, and the bench becomes a horizontally scrolling shape tray plus compact color/edit groups. Touch gestures orbit and pinch-zoom the stage; shape selection followed by tap avoids requiring drag-and-drop on touch.

**Originality and portability:** Geometry and scenery are assembled from primitives at runtime. There are no branded characters, imported set designs, stock illustrations, or third-party audio. Portable rules and snapshot validation live separately from Babylon rendering and browser storage; no platform shell is bundled.

## Metro interiors: stay with the journey
A nonmodal cutaway below the city keeps dispatch available while opening a platform or carriage. Pale-green structure, ivory floors, seafoam windows, operational coral/blue trim and an overhead destination sign extend the coastal world. Small original passenger figures use live IDs/counts, bounded to 24 with an explicit remaining count. A readable broadcast rail pairs captions with play/retry, mute and status. Mobile stacks scene and broadcast without horizontal page overflow. Keyboard station/train activation, focus restoration and Escape support exploration without trapping focus.

Real-service captions are kept separate from a clearly labeled broadcast studio for hypothetical non-stopping-service and sample notices. Recorded full sentences retain one voice per spoken language across devices; captions and failure states never disappear when audio is unavailable. Automatic journey updates finish the active sentence and keep only the newest pending announcement, which starts on completion even without another simulation event. A separate pending line leaves the main caption aligned with the speaking recording. Explicit context/replay/language changes may replace audio, but mute, close and page exit clear the queue. Reusing one media element keeps the mobile authorization path consistent. Snapshot and playback seams leave room for richer renderers and refined recordings without modifying dispatch simulation.
